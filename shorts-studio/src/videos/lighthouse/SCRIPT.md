# "The Night the Lighthouse Went Dark" — Master Script & Beat Sheet
Format: Shorts-Studio v2 vertical cartoon 3D short (1080x1920, 30 fps, ~3.7 words/s).
Premise: a rotating (flashing) light warns ships off the rocks, a steady light marks a harbour entrance. When the
rotation motor dies in a storm, the keeper's stuck beam turns into an invitation, so the safest light is no light.
Original fictional story (not a historical account).

## Voice & Dialogue Script
[NARRATOR] A lighthouse keeper turns off his light in the year's worst storm, and saves every ship.
[NARRATOR] It's midnight, and waves are smashing the rocks below his tower while twelve cargo ships crawl toward the harbor.
[NARRATOR] His one job is simple: keep the big lamp spinning, because a flashing light means danger, rocks, stay away.
[NARRATOR] Then lightning hits the tower, and the motor that turns the lamp dies.
[NARRATOR] Now the light just sits there, steady and bright.
[NARRATOR] He cranks it by hand, he kicks it, he even begs it.
[KEEPER] "Come on, spin, you rusty old thing!"
[NARRATOR] Nothing. And out at sea, the first ship is already turning straight toward him.
[CAPTAIN] "Steady light ahead, that's the harbor! Full speed!"
[NARRATOR] You see, to a sailor, a flashing light says keep away, but a steady one says come on in.
[NARRATOR] His broken lighthouse is inviting twelve ships onto the rocks.
[NARRATOR] So he grabs the big switch, and pulls it.
[NARRATOR] Total darkness. Twelve confused captains drop anchor and wait for morning.
[NARRATOR] At sunrise, every ship is still floating, and every captain files the same angry complaint.
[CAPTAIN] "Your keeper left us in the dark!"
[NARRATOR] He did. It's the only reason they're alive to complain.
