# "What If You Fell Into a Black Hole?" — Master Script & Beat Sheet
Format: Shorts-Studio v2 vertical cartoon 3D short (1080x1920, 30 fps, ~3.7 words/s).
Science context: gravitational time dilation and redshift seen by a distant observer, the "firewall" / black-hole
complementarity picture (outside observers see infalling matter thermalised at the stretched horizon, while the
infaller crosses a large horizon smoothly), and tidal spaghettification near the singularity.

## Beats
1. Hook — paradox stated as fact (you die twice).
2. Setting — astronaut drifting toward the black hole; friend on the ship with a telescope.
3. Beat — time dilation: the friend sees the astronaut's clock slow, light redden, freeze at the horizon.
4. Beat — the friend sees a wall of radiation burn the astronaut to ash at the horizon.
5. Beat — from the astronaut's side: nothing at all at the horizon.
6. Beat — deep inside: spaghettification.
7. Pivot ("You see,") — nobody can compare both stories, so both stay true.
8. Payoff — two funerals; ends on "noodle".

## Voice & Dialogue Script
[NARRATOR] Fall into a black hole, and you die twice, and both of those deaths are completely true.
[NARRATOR] You're an astronaut, drifting toward a black sphere so heavy it bends the starlight around it like a lens.
[NARRATOR] Its edge is called the event horizon, and past it, not even light can climb back out.
[NARRATOR] Your friend stays safely on the ship, watching you through a telescope.
[FRIEND] "Uh, your clock is ticking slower. Way slower."
[ASTRONAUT] "Mine's fine! Totally normal!"
[NARRATOR] To her, you turn redder, dimmer, and slower, until you freeze right at the edge, like a photo.
[NARRATOR] Then a blazing wall of radiation flares up, and she watches you burn into ash, right there on the surface.
[NARRATOR] But from your side? Nothing. You float straight through the horizon. No wall, no fire, not even a bump.
[ASTRONAUT] "Huh. I thought this would hurt."
[NARRATOR] It does, later. Deep inside, gravity pulls your feet harder than your head, and you stretch like spaghetti.
[NARRATOR] You see, nobody can ever stand on both sides to compare notes, so the universe never has to pick one story.
[NARRATOR] Which means your friend holds a funeral for a pile of ash, while you're still in there, becoming a noodle.
